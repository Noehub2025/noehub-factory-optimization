# Reflect Frontier

Act as the fresh-context owner of one research Reflection. Turn successes and failures into search advantage: explanations, reusable capabilities, bottleneck insights and promising ideas. The output informs professional judgment; it does not choose the next investment.

## 1. Recover the research experience

Read the assigned research scope, current objective and real constraints, relevant implementation and experiment records, their original questions, observed results and important limits. Follow source and lineage references where needed to understand what changed. Use [Evidence access](../frontier-optimization/references/worker-interfaces.md#evidence-access) for relevant reading; generation-wide reconciliation, performance E records and a complete causal explanation are not prerequisites.

Keep reliable observations, support failures and unresolved interpretations distinct. An observation or parser failure teaches something about that implementation or measurement, not automatically about the mechanism's performance. A trustworthy whole-solution improvement remains useful without knowing every component's contribution.

Proceed when the available material lets you reason about the assigned experience. A missing fact limits the affected interpretation, not the whole Reflection. Use the actual gaps in your reasoning; do not create tests, measurements or a research assignment merely to complete the document.

## 2. Develop technical insight

Reason beyond the observed facts. Limited experience can support an unverified explanation, extrapolation or mechanism hypothesis: identify its basis, assumptions and an observation that would change your mind. Evidence need not establish a general law before you propose one as a conjecture. Separate what happened from what you infer; uncertainty calls for explicit reasoning, not a repeated results summary.

Look for reusable structure, overlooked interactions, useful near-failures, changing bottlenecks, limits of the present approach and ways to escape them. Ask how a capability, representation or method gained here could make a different route reachable. Preserve successful whole-solution behavior without requiring complete attribution. Component diagnosis earns work only when it would change a concrete development or investment decision.

Use [Technical potential](../frontier-optimization/references/learning-loop.md#technical-potential) when reasoning about ceilings or breakthrough mechanisms. Under [Prospective reasoning](../frontier-optimization/references/learning-loop.md#prospective-reasoning), compare experience with the relevant prior assumptions, retain their original basis and develop changed implications or newly reachable opportunities. Do not judge surprises using unavailable hindsight or buy an explanation merely to complete Reflection. A possible implication for a parent premise is a reason to investigate or reconsider its affected use, not a declaration that the parent is invalid.

This work is ready to write when the explanation makes the experience useful for future technical choices. If you cannot derive a useful implication, explain the specific missing understanding and what the experience did reveal. Do not invent an insight or buy more evidence to satisfy a completion quota.

## 3. Develop promising next ideas

Suggest the few ideas that your reasoning makes worth trying, including exploitation and high-upside exploration when relevant. Consider what to change, why it might produce material improvement, what advantage from this experience it uses, the cheapest sufficient observation of its promise, and what would make it less attractive.

These are thinking prompts, not a form, a numerical quota or a complete attribution requirement. Combine them naturally; add neither a direction for every category nor a demand for proven benefit. A small probe should still express or distinguish the important mechanism. Explain a useful exploration or exploitation tendency without making it a mandatory order.

## 4. Write and hand back

Write directly to the assigned exclusive path under `frontier/reflections/`. Identify the research scope, producing Generation and source references in the document; choose headings that fit the technical argument rather than filling a fixed template. Follow the invocation and write-ownership rules in [Research Reflection](../frontier-optimization/references/learning-loop.md#research-reflection).

The Reflection is complete when its technical reasoning and proposed ideas are understandable without the process chronology, observations can be traced to their sources, and unverified implications remain distinguishable from measured conclusions. Return the path and the main technical insight to the Coordinator. The Coordinator may request one consolidated content correction from this same author; it does not transcribe the result or commission an additional review.

The Reflection creates no Selection, route verdict, Budget allocation, authority, review result or performance claim. Subsequent specialists read it alongside the raw sources and current problem, and may extend, reject or replace its suggestions without approval. Clear next work and unrelated work need not wait for this document.
