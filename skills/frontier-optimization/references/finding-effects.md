# Frontier finding effects

Read when interpreting a validator or review finding, not before unrelated planning or research.

## Finding effects

This section is the sole semantic owner of finding effects. Validators derive the effect from a stable code through `finding_effects.py`; callers and reviewers cannot supply or weaken it. An unknown code defaults to `block`.

For a human finding, judge fitness for the current use, not freedom from defects. Connect the credible triggering conditions to the practical consequence, considering existing limits or recovery and the cost of repair and delayed useful feedback. Explain only what matters to the disposition in the existing finding; this is not a per-defect form, probability estimate or proof of zero risk. A real or constructible failure alone does not require repair. An incident need not have happened for a serious consequence to matter, and low probability alone does not excuse violating a real boundary.

Pause the affected action when correction is needed to respect actual authority or constraints, prevent material harm, or support the observation needed for the current decision. Otherwise continue within an already sufficient use limit, or leave a limited-impact defect unfixed when repair would displace more useful work. The latter may apply to a defect reachable in current use; it requires neither a new safeguard nor a claim that the defect cannot occur. Novelty, uncertain benefit and disagreement with internal wording do not establish a blocker. Machine-coded findings retain their effects until the affected requirement or implementation is corrected; callers cannot relabel them.

For source or usage concerns, first establish the current requirement through [Constrain effects, not convenient forms](technical-design.md#constrain-effects-not-convenient-forms). Supported use proceeds under its applicable conditions. Resolve an answerable doubt through a targeted check in the same task. If evidence genuinely necessary for that use remains unknown, pause only dependent use and continue information gathering or unaffected work; this neither grants permission nor rejects the route. A confirmed applicable prohibition stops the affected use. Missing an internally preferred complete chain, without a concrete consequential doubt, is not itself a necessary-evidence gap or route failure.

When corrected evidence defeats a blocking premise, update the current disposition and its actual enforcing definition or code through [Repair an existing execution restriction](batch-current.md#repair-an-existing-execution-restriction), preserving historical records and unaffected conclusions. A narrative correction alone does not clear a machine failure, and a reviewer cannot downgrade its coded effect. Correct the affected dependency and check that path; do not automatically restart the full review chain or reopen investment unless the next investment actually changes.

For a threshold finding, first resolve the current owner, applicability, and maximum consequence through [Decision-bearing thresholds](frontier-core.md#decision-bearing-thresholds). A stable finding code decides the readiness of the affected transition; it does not establish the scope of a technical conclusion. That conclusion cannot exceed both the threshold's allowed consequence and [the scope supported by the negative evidence](frontier-core.md#scope-of-negative-implementation-evidence). Violating a verified applicable external, safety, or resource boundary blocks only the affected action. Violating a current W implementation-acceptance threshold rejects only the affected realization unless evidence supports a broader scope. Exceeding a regression line or diagnostic records an observation and may trigger local investigation; it does not establish `technical no-path`. An unsupported internal constant pauses only the judgment that depends on it and returns to its current owner for calibration. If that constant claims to protect an external, safety, or irreversible boundary, pause the affected action while verifying the real boundary, without eliminating the route or blocking unrelated work. A downstream transcription or binding mismatch makes only the derived check unusable and receives `repair`; the subject remains unchanged.

- `block`: the current action is unsafe or ambiguous. Preserve evidence and stop the affected transition. The effect does not by itself show that parent meaning changed or require a new B, user authorization, parent review, or rerun. Use one of those only when the semantic object, a prior answer, or an actual effect must change.
- `repair`: the current serialization, derived field, or transient realization is unusable, but the authoritative objective, evidence, and maximum consequence are unchanged and mechanically provable. Readiness remains false until correction. Repair inside the same semantic B and V before review or through an already authorized boundary-preserving continuation; rerun only the affected deterministic checks. A changed reviewed semantic input still requires the applicable review of that version, using [Entry repair review](entry-review.md#entry-repair-review) for an Entry correction.
- `advisory`: the current use remains sufficiently supported despite a suggestion or known limited-impact defect, including one reachable in current use. Readiness remains true without requiring its repair, another safeguard or re-review. Preserve the defect and any relevant use limit; acceptance is not evidence that the behavior is correct. A defect requiring correction under the decision-impact test above remains `repair` or `block`. Validators keep their coded effects; a reviewer cannot relabel a machine error to bypass it.

A timestamp that controls a deadline, ordering, lifecycle transition, or authorization is never advisory. A digest that binds candidate, evaluator, evidence, target, authority, or reproducibility is never advisory. A narrative disagreement is advisory only when `current_state`, its cited ledger decision, and every typed authority projection agree; otherwise it is `block`. Keep advisories out of strength, validity, progress, and constraint conclusions.

An advisory may remain unfixed. Revisit it in ordinary work when its impact or repair value changes, without reopening unaffected conclusions. Retain the reviewed version; changed behavior still needs its relevant checks under current rules. An advisory creates no replacement object or approval.

Historical artifacts retain their original validator and review meaning. Apply this contract prospectively; do not rewrite or mass-migrate old B, V, R, E, OR, or X records merely because finding output gained effects.

Throughout Frontier, `finding-free` means zero `block` or `repair` findings. Advisories are reported separately and do not make a ready object nonpositive.
